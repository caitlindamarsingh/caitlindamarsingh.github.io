PROJECT 02 / Mechanism design · Manufacturing planning

Turning rotation into a slicing stroke.

I evaluated a slotted-lever mechanism for converting an air engine’s rotation into a guided slicing stroke.

ROLEConcept evaluation & manufacturing planning · EML2322L
CONTEXTSpring 2026
STATUSProposed design
IN THIS PROJECTThe problemMy roleDesign processResults & statusProject gallery

01 / THE PROBLEM

Design requirements.

I worked on a fruit-slicer concept with a 5-inch reciprocating blade stroke, adjustable slice thickness, and a feed arrangement intended to keep hands away from the cutting area.

02 / MY ROLE

My contribution.

I described the mechanism, specified component materials and manufacturing methods, and estimated parts costs for the proposed slicer.

03 / DESIGN PROCESS

My design process.

  1. 01

    Convert rotation to linear motion

    I evaluated a slotted linkage driven by the flywheel’s crank pin. A second slot transfers the linkage’s motion to the blade carriage.

  2. 02

    Guide the blade and feed the fruit

    I considered how rectangular guideways could constrain the carriage, while a slider feeds the fruit and an adjustable wall sets slice thickness.

  3. 03

    Plan manufacturing

    I specified 6061 aluminum for the structure, Delrin for sliding components, PETG for the holder and base, and stainless steel for the blade.

Design challenges

I considered carriage alignment, blade retention, material selection, and separation of hands from the cutting region. Operating safety and food-contact suitability would need validation before use.
