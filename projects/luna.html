PROJECT 03 / Rocket design · Simulation

Designing toward a 2,000-foot flight.

I designed Luna V1 in OpenRocket, refining its airframe, fins, and motor setup toward a stable certification flight.

CONTEXTPreliminary design review

01 / THE PROBLEM

Design requirements.

I evaluated a single-stage rocket concept for a stable flight to approximately 2,000 feet and recovery by parachute, as part of my preparation for Level 1 high-power rocketry certification.

02 / MY ROLE

My contribution.

I designed the rocket in OpenRocket and evaluated vehicle sizing, materials, motor configuration, recovery, and simulated performance. My Level 1 certification is in progress.

03 / DESIGN PROCESS

My design process.

  1. 01

    Define the targets

    I used a 2,000-foot apogee target and a nominal 15 ft/s descent target to guide the design evaluation.

  2. 02

    Compare materials

    In the preliminary design, I compared strength, impact resistance, cost, and density using weighted decision matrices. I continued refining the vehicle in OpenRocket, including its fins and motor configuration.

  3. 03

    Compare design iterations

    I used OpenRocket to evaluate altitude, stability, and recovery as the design changed. I keep each prediction tied to its model configuration so results from different iterations are not treated as interchangeable.

Tools & materials

  • OpenRocket
  • Material decision matrices
  • Blue Tube airframe
  • Fin and motor configuration
  • Parachute recovery

Design challenges

I weighed stability and structural requirements against mass, cost, and recovery speed. Material selection was a key part of balancing those competing needs.

04 / RESULTS & STATUS

Results & status.

The current model predicts an apogee of about 1,519 feet, below my 2,000-foot target. The earlier preliminary-design study predicted 2,206 feet with a 2.13-caliber stability margin. These are results from different design iterations, not flight measurements.
